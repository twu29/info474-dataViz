Introduction
The Olympic Games have grown from a small gathering of athletes in Athens in 1896 to a massive global event featuring thousands of competitors from over 200 nations. Along the way, the Games have reflected shifting geopolitics, evolving notions of gender equality, and changing demographics in elite sport.

In this article, we explore three core questions about Olympic participation and performance using data spanning from the early 1900s to the modern era. We begin with a broad view of how medal counts have shifted across countries over time, then zoom into the age profiles of gold medalists, and finally examine how participation has changed across gender and geography.

Age distributions of gold medalists at Olympic Games
The youngest athlete to win a gold medal achieved this feat at 13 years old. The age of medaling reveals the peaks of athletic performance across different sports and eras of the Olympics where the most optimal training regimines and experiences have evolved. Exploring the age distribution of medalists uncovers patterns of how age and achievement intersect.

In an average distribution of medalists across age ranges in the Summer Olympics, we can see that athletes aged 22-24 are most likely to win medals. This age range also highlights the most gold medalists, with a significant drop-off in medal counts as athletes age, especially in ages over 30. Ages 19-30 have the highest concentration of medalists, suggesting that this is the most successful range for Olympic performance.
